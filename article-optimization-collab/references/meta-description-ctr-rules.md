# Meta Description CTR Rules

Use these rules whenever an article package creates or revises a Meta Description. They combine the WordPress blog format's `main keyword + practical outcome + trust/clarity hook` pattern with the Meta Tags Optimizer's AIDA, PAS, and Benefit-Proof-CTA frameworks.

## Core Principle

A Meta Description is SERP ad copy, not a neutral article synopsis. It should make the right searcher feel that clicking this result will resolve a specific problem, save effort or money, reduce uncertainty, or reveal a useful comparison.

Use this default structure:

`Search intent/result + concrete benefit or differentiator + proof/clarity hook + action`

## Required Ingredients

- Use the primary keyword naturally, preferably in the first half.
- Answer the actual query intent: access, price, comparison, tutorial, limits, safety, or purchase decision.
- Promise one concrete outcome, not a vague `learn more` benefit.
- Add one specific differentiator or proof point that is genuinely present in the page: tested routes, current pricing, model comparison, official-vs-platform distinction, exact number of options, step-by-step workflow, or another verified advantage.
- Close with a natural action or curiosity trigger such as `compare the routes`, `see which plan wins`, `find the fastest option`, `check the real limits`, or `choose before you pay`.
- Preserve the user's direct commercial voice when the page supports it. Accuracy comes from precise facts and attribution, not bland wording.

## Frameworks

Choose the framework that matches the URL's intent:

1. **Benefit-Proof-CTA**: Lead with the outcome, support it with a concrete differentiator or evidence, then prompt the click.
2. **PAS**: Name the reader's friction, sharpen the cost or inconvenience, then present the practical route and action.
3. **AIDA**: Open with a strong relevant hook, build interest with specificity, create desire with a real benefit, and close with action.
4. **Comparison**: State the decision, name the criteria, and promise a clear winner or best-fit answer.
5. **Pricing**: Surface the real cost question, mention limits or hidden tradeoffs, and invite comparison before purchase.

## Length and Output

- English target: aim for 145-160 characters and never exceed 160.
- Chinese target: usually aim for about 70-80 Chinese characters while checking the rendered snippet.
- Count the final characters, including spaces and punctuation.
- Produce three genuinely different options: `intent-safe`, `benefit-led`, and `conversion-led`.
- For each option, report the character count, framework, CTA, and emotional/CTR hook.
- Recommend one winner based on the target URL's intent; do not present three near-duplicates without a decision.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not use generic summary openings such as:

- `This article explains...`
- `In this guide, you will learn...`
- `Learn everything about...`
- `Discover the features of...`
- `We cover pricing, features, and FAQs...`

Also avoid:

- repeating the title without adding a new reason to click;
- listing section names with no outcome;
- vague hype such as `ultimate`, `revolutionary`, or `best ever` without page evidence;
- unsupported urgency, scarcity, prices, savings, or guarantees;
- stale model names, years, prices, or access routes;
- stuffing every keyword at the expense of a natural sentence.

## Intent Templates

Use these as structures, not fixed copy:

- **How to/access**: `Get [product] through [routes] without [friction]. Compare [price/limits/differences] and find the fastest practical option.`
- **Pricing**: `See what [product] really costs, where the limits sit, and which route gives you more value. Compare the options before you pay.`
- **Comparison**: `[A] or [B]? See who wins on [criteria] and which option fits [audience/use case] before you commit.`
- **Tutorial**: `Create [result] with [product] using [specific workflow]. Follow the practical steps, avoid [common failure], and get a stronger result.`
- **Alternatives**: `Need a better [category] option? Compare [number] alternatives on [criteria] and find the best fit for [benefit/use case].`

## QA Gate

Before handoff, verify:

- the description matches the URL's real search intent;
- the promised benefit is delivered near the top of the page;
- the main keyword reads naturally;
- the differentiator is specific and supported by the page;
- the wording creates a reason to click beyond the title;
- the CTA is natural rather than generic;
- the final count is within the language-specific limit;
- model names, year, pricing, availability, and product routes are current;
- the recommended option is clearly identified in the workbench.
